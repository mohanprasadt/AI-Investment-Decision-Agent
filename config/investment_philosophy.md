# Investment Philosophy Configuration

## Investor Profile

**Markets:** Canadian stocks (primary), US stocks (primary), Indian stocks (occasional)
**Style:** Long-term investor, not an aggressive trader
**Approach:** Blended value + growth + dividend investing
**Primary Objective:** Balanced long-term growth and income

---

## Core Principle

A great company is not automatically a great investment at any price.
A cheap stock is not automatically a good investment.

The preferred intersection:
> High-quality business + durable competitive advantage + sustainable growth +
> strong financials + reasonable valuation + acceptable portfolio fit.

---

## Business Quality Preferences

- High-quality, understandable businesses
- Sustainable growth
- Durable competitive advantages (moats)
- Consistent revenue growth
- Strong financial quality
- Consistently good ROIC
- Low to no debt
- Reasonable valuation
- Sustainable dividends where appropriate
- Gradual accumulation preferred over large one-time purchases
- Emerging companies acceptable if: strong growth, low debt, credible advantage, improving economics

---

## Investment Decision Sequence

1. Understand the business
2. Understand the industry
3. Evaluate business quality
4. Evaluate competitive advantage / moat
5. Evaluate growth quality
6. Evaluate financial quality
7. Evaluate dividend quality (where applicable)
8. Evaluate management
9. Identify risks
10. Estimate fair value
11. Evaluate current valuation
12. Evaluate portfolio fit
13. Construct bull case
14. Construct bear case
15. Identify thesis breakers
16. Determine decision
17. Determine accumulation strategy
18. Establish monitoring criteria

**Do not allow valuation to dominate analysis prematurely.**

---

## Moat Evaluation Framework

### Possible Moat Sources
- Network effects
- Switching costs
- Cost advantage / scale
- Brand (must demonstrate pricing power, not just recognition)
- Intellectual property
- Regulatory barriers
- Efficient scale
- Ecosystem / platform effects
- Distribution advantage
- Data advantage

### Evaluation Method
**Claim → Evidence → Durability → Threats → Conclusion**

### Moat Classification
| Dimension     | Options                                         |
|---------------|-------------------------------------------------|
| Strength      | None / Weak / Moderate / Strong / Exceptional  |
| Durability    | Low / Medium / High                             |
| Trajectory    | Strengthening / Stable / Weakening              |

**Brand recognition alone does NOT qualify as a moat** unless it demonstrably produces:
pricing power, superior margins, customer loyalty, retention, or switching costs.

---

## Evidence Hierarchy

| Tier | Sources |
|------|---------|
| Tier 1 (Primary) | SEC filings, SEDAR+, Annual reports, Quarterly reports, Investor presentations, Regulatory disclosures |
| Tier 2 (Secondary) | Reputable financial databases, established financial publications, industry research |
| Tier 3 (Supporting) | Investor commentary, forums, Reddit, social media |

Tier 3 may identify concerns and research leads but must NOT be treated as authoritative financial evidence.

---

## Decision Output Categories

| Decision | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| START | High-quality opportunity with reasonable valuation |
| START SMALL | Attractive but meaningful uncertainty or portfolio considerations |
| ACCUMULATE | Existing/new position; attractive valuation, intact thesis |
| HOLD | Existing position; thesis intact but insufficient reason to add |
| WATCH | High-quality company but valuation not sufficiently attractive |
| AVOID | Risk/reward unattractive or business quality insufficient |
| REDUCE | Thesis weakened or portfolio considerations warrant reduction |
| EXIT / THESIS BROKEN | Material deterioration in investment thesis |

---

## Existing Holdings: Position Actions

- New investment
- Add to position
- Accumulate
- Hold
- Watch
- Reduce
- Exit

Compare original thesis vs current fundamentals. Explicitly identify: **What changed?**

---

## Accumulation Zones

Do not apply a universal fixed percentage discount for all companies.
Margin of safety must reflect: business quality, predictability, moat, financial strength, growth uncertainty, valuation confidence.

| Zone | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| WAIT | Price materially above reasonable valuation |
| WATCH | Attractive business, valuation not compelling |
| START SMALL | Reasonably attractive entry point |
| ACCUMULATE | Attractive valuation with sufficient margin of safety |

Lower-quality or higher-uncertainty companies require a larger margin of safety.

---

## Confidence Levels

Every major conclusion must carry a confidence level:
- **High** — Strong data, consistent signals, reliable assumptions
- **Medium** — Adequate data, some uncertainty
- **Low** — Limited data, significant uncertainty, conflicting signals

Confidence reflects quality of analysis, NOT probability the stock will rise.

---

## Mandatory Devil's Advocate / Bear Case

After forming a preliminary thesis, deliberately attempt to disprove it:
- Search for negative developments, competitive threats, weak trends
- Ask: *What evidence would make me NOT buy this company?*
- Evaluate whether bear case materially challenges the thesis
- Never allow confirmation bias to dominate research

---

## AI Design Principles

1. Avoid confirmation bias
2. Separate facts from interpretation
3. Identify and communicate uncertainty
4. Challenge preliminary conclusions
5. Avoid false precision in valuation
6. Avoid over-reliance on P/E alone
7. Avoid equating dividend yield with dividend quality
8. Avoid equating brand recognition with moat
9. Treat portfolio allocation as a guideline, not a rigid constraint
10. Treat quantitative thresholds as guidelines, not absolute laws
11. Explain exceptions to guidelines
12. Maintain an audit trail of important decisions
