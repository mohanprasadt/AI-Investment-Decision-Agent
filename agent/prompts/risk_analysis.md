# Risk Analysis Research Template

## Purpose
Identify and characterize the risks that could impair the investment thesis. Risk analysis is not pessimism — it is the discipline of knowing what you're accepting when you invest.

## Critical Rule
**Classify every risk.** Is this risk temporary (will likely resolve), structural (permanent change to the business model), or uncertain (cannot yet determine)? The classification changes what you do about it.

## Research Prompt

**Ticker:** `{ticker}`
**Industry:** `{industry_name}`
**Moat strength (from moat analysis):** `{moat_strength}`

---

### Step 1 — Business risks

Risks specific to this company's operations and business model:

For each risk, state:
```
Description: [clear description]
Type: TEMPORARY / STRUCTURAL / UNCERTAIN
Severity: High / Medium / Low
Likelihood: High / Medium / Low
Mitigants: [what reduces this risk]
Evidence: [source and tier]
```

Categories to investigate:
- Key customer concentration (top customer > 20% of revenue?)
- Key person risk (CEO departure would materially change the investment case?)
- Technology obsolescence risk
- Input cost volatility (commodity exposure, labor, energy)
- Demand cyclicality
- Product/service liability risk
- Debt maturity risk (near-term refinancing needs?)

---

### Step 2 — Competitive risks

Risks from the competitive environment:

- New entrants: Is there a well-funded competitor entering the market?
- Disruptive technology: Is AI, automation, or another technology threatening the core product?
- Pricing pressure: Is there evidence of margin compression from competition?
- Market share trends: Is this company gaining or losing market share?

---

### Step 3 — Macro / external risks

Risks from the broader environment:

- Currency risk (revenue in one currency, costs in another?)
- Interest rate sensitivity (floating rate debt?)
- Commodity input exposure
- Geopolitical risk (revenue from politically unstable regions?)
- Regulatory risk (pending regulation that could impair the business?)
- ESG / carbon transition risk (if industry is fossil-fuel dependent)

---

### Step 4 — Financial risks

- Leverage: Debt-to-EBITDA > 3x? Near-term covenant risk?
- Liquidity: FCF sufficient to cover debt service and dividends?
- Pension obligations: Underfunded?
- Off-balance-sheet exposure: Operating leases, guarantees, litigation?

---

### Step 5 — Overall risk classification

| Level | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| `LOW` | No material structural risks; temporary risks are manageable and mitigated |
| `MODERATE` | Some risks present but manageable; at least one area of monitoring needed |
| `HIGH` | One or more structural or high-severity risks that require careful position sizing |

**Risk summary:** [2–3 sentences]

---

### Evidence requirements

- Factual risk items should cite source and tier
- Risk severity and likelihood are interpretive (L2) — label them as such
- Structural risks require more rigorous evidence than temporary risks

---

### What NOT to do

- Do NOT list generic industry risks that apply to every company (that is not analysis)
- Do NOT rate all risks as "medium" to avoid commitment
- Do NOT assess valuation here
- Do NOT make investment recommendations
