# Management Assessment Research Template

## Purpose
Evaluate the quality, integrity, and track record of the management team. Great businesses with poor management can destroy capital. Strong management in a mediocre business can create unexpected value.

## Research Prompt

**Ticker:** `{ticker}`
**CEO:** `{ceo_name}` (if known)
**Years in role:** `{ceo_tenure}` (if known)

---

### Step 1 — Capital allocation track record

Capital allocation is the most important management skill for long-term investors. Assess:

**Reinvestment discipline:**
- What return on incremental capital has management achieved on reinvested earnings?
- Has ROIC been stable, improving, or declining over their tenure?

**M&A track record:**
- Has the company made acquisitions? If so, did they create or destroy value?
- Did management overpay? Did the integration succeed?

**Share buybacks:**
- Has the company bought back shares? Was timing good (bought below fair value)?
- Or have buybacks been at peaks (value destructive)?

**Dividends:**
- If dividends are paid, is the payout ratio sustainable?
- Has the dividend been maintained and grown through cycles?

**Debt usage:**
- Has management used debt wisely or dangerously?
- Did they take on debt during low-rate environments and now face maturity risk?

---

### Step 2 — Integrity and alignment signals

- Is management's compensation well-aligned with shareholder outcomes?
- Have there been material related-party transactions?
- Have there been accounting restatements or regulatory investigations?
- How has management communicated in tough quarters — honest or spin?
- Do they acknowledge mistakes or deflect?

**Founder / owner-operator note:**
- Is this a founder-led company? (typically positive for long-term thinking)
- Do insiders own meaningful stakes? (alignment indicator)
- Has insider ownership been increasing or decreasing?

---

### Step 3 — Communication quality

- Does management set realistic guidance and deliver on it?
- Is shareholder communication clear and direct?
- Do annual report letters focus on long-term value or short-term optics?
- Have they been transparent about challenges?

---

### Step 4 — Overall rating

| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| `STRONG` | Excellent capital allocation track record, aligned incentives, honest communication, no red flags |
| `GOOD` | Good track record with minor concerns; above-average capital discipline |
| `MIXED` | Reasonable operators but some capital allocation concerns or communication gaps |
| `WEAK` | Poor capital allocation, poor track record, misaligned incentives, or integrity concerns |
| `INSUFFICIENT_DATA` | Cannot assess without more information |

**Management summary:** [2–3 sentences synthesizing the above]

---

### Evidence requirements

- Capital allocation facts should come from financial statements (Tier 1)
- Track record assessments from annual reports and earnings transcripts (Tier 1/2)
- Insider ownership from regulatory filings (Tier 1)
- Opinions about management quality must be labeled L2 (interpretation)

---

### What NOT to do

- Do NOT rate management based on stock price performance alone (correlation ≠ causation)
- Do NOT conflate a great business with great management — the business may succeed despite management
- Do NOT make valuation assessments here
- Do NOT make investment recommendations
